Digital technologies and business innovation are transforming the way individuals work, communicate, learn, and access economic opportunities. Advances in artificial intelligence, fintech, digital platforms, and data-driven decision making are reshaping industries and labor markets worldwide, while also creating new challenges related to inequality, productivity, ethics, regulation, and access to technology.

This research agenda examines how technological change and business innovation affect organizations, labor markets, financial systems, and economic development. Current projects explore topics such as digital transformation, financial technology (fintech), artificial intelligence, organizational ethics, gender inclusion in leadership, and the role of technology and innovation in promoting inclusive and sustainable economic growth.

Estrategia, microeconomía e IA Cómo diseñar sistemas de decisión con estrategia, riesgo y accountabilityFor decades, microeconomics has helped business leaders navigate pricing, costs, incentives, risk, and competition. Today, many of those decisions happen beyond the boardroom—embedded in algorithms, predictive models, and automated rules that run thousands of times a day.

The challenge is not the adoption of artificial intelligence. It is the automation of decisions before anyone has designed the economic logic behind them.

Strategy, Microeconomics, and AI advances a powerful argument: competitive advantage depends on building better decision systems, not simply better models. A model predicts. A system decides. And every automated decision embodies an economic theory, whether its designers recognize it or not.

This book offers a framework for turning microeconomics into an operating system for organizational decision-making—one that brings together strategy, risk, traceability, accountability, and disciplined learning. It moves beyond explaining theory to help leaders redesign how their organizations make decisions in a world where AI increasingly acts on their behalf.

VelaFi: Competing Against Giants in the Stablecoin Payments RaceIn early 2026, VelaFi CEO Maggie Wu faces a make-or-break decision. Her Mexico-based startup has already shown that stablecoins can make cross-border payments in Latin America faster, cheaper, and more transparent. Now global giants are moving in: Stripe has acquired Bridge for $1.1 billion, PayPal has launched its own stablecoin, and major banks are rapidly expanding into crypto services.

With limited capital and little time, VelaFi must decide how to survive—and where it can still win. Should it specialise in international payroll or treasury services, focus on underserved Latin American SMEs, or compete through service and local expertise that larger firms cannot easily replicate?

This case explores how a resource-constrained startup can defend its position amid technological disruption, regulatory uncertainty, and intensifying competition in the fast-changing world of stablecoins and emerging-market finance.

Project 1: Impact of Government Digitalization on Business: Evidence from Estonia's Digital Transformation

As governments worldwide invest heavily in e-government infrastructure, a critical empirical question is whether digital public administration translates into measurable gains for firms and the broader economy. Using synthetic control methods, this project evaluates Estonia's landmark digitalization reforms — including the X-Road interoperability platform launched in the early 2000s — against a carefully constructed counterfactual of comparable transition and European economies. Project 2:Bridging the Fintech Gap: Regulatory Frictions and Active Financial Inclusion in an Emerging Economy

As digital financial infrastructure expands across emerging markets, a critical question remains unresolved: under what conditions does fintech development translate into spcially impactful financial inclusion rather than mere formal access? This study examines Mexico's fintech ecosystem — one of Latin America's largest and most dynamic — through the lens of a structural paradox: advanced regulatory and technological infrastructure coexisting with persistently low adoption rates and deep inclusion gaps. Drawing on thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews with key stakeholders across Mexico's digital financial ecosystem — including regulators, fintech entrepreneurs, incumbent financial institutions, and financial inclusion specialists — the study identifies structural frictions that explain the divergence between infrastructure development and active inclusionUP Researcher: Eugenio GómezLaura Jiménez CasillasAndrée Marie López-Fernández

How to Effectively Communicate Your Code of Ethics: Experimental Evidence from Mexico

Can companies improve ethical behavior simply by communicating and explaining their code of ethics to employees? This study evaluates the effectiveness of an ethics program implemented at one of the largest pawnshop chains in Mexico using a cluster randomized controlled trial involving 519 workers.

The findings show that additional communication and explanation of the company’s existing code of ethics did not significantly improve employees’ understanding of ethical standards. The study highlights that some corporate ethics programs may be ineffective despite substantial investments and demonstrates how rigorous experimental methods can be applied to evaluate business practices and organizational behavior.

The Perfect Fit: How to Get the Best Talent in Executive Positions

Hiring decisions for leadership positions can be influenced not only by qualifications and experience, but also by social perceptions and biases related to gender, family status, and parenthood. This study examines how these factors shape evaluations of candidates applying for executive positions in Mexico.

Using an experimental design with fictional resumes, the research explores whether decision-makers evaluate candidates differently depending on characteristics such as sex, marital status, and parenthood. The study contributes to broader discussions on workplace equality, discrimination, and talent selection in upper management and highlights the importance of fair and inclusive hiring practices in professional environments.
