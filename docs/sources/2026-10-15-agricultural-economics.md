Agricultural and environmental economics examine how households, firms, and governments make decisions about land, natural resources, food systems, and rural development. Agriculture remains central to livelihoods, poverty reduction, climate adaptation, and environmental sustainability, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. At the same time, land degradation, water scarcity, insecure property rights, and climate shocks continue to shape productivity and long-term welfare.

This research agenda studies how institutions, incentives, and environmental conditions affect agricultural investment, conservation, and rural development.

Current Projects

Project 1: Land Tenure and Soil Conservation in Peru: Evidence from Double/Debiased Machine Learning

Farmers often make long-term investments in soil conservation only when they feel secure about their land and future livelihoods. This project examines whether land ownership and formal land titles encourage farmers in Peru to adopt conservation practices that protect soil quality and improve the sustainability of agricultural production. Using nationwide agricultural census data, the research studies practices such as intercropping, organic fertilizer use, and traditional conservation infrastructure including andenes and terraces. The project also explores how geography, local institutions, and environmental conditions shape conservation decisions across different regions of Peru.UP Researcher: Esteban Colla‐De‐Robertis and Akito Kameiwith Sandro Navarro-Castañeda (Universidad de Piura)Presentation at Asociación Peruana de Economía (APE) in 2026.

Land tenure security and agrarian investments in the Peruvian Highlands

Secure land ownership is often considered an important foundation for long-term agricultural investment and rural development. This project examines whether land tenure security encourages farmers in the Peruvian Highlands to invest in agricultural production and land improvement practices. Using data from the Peruvian Agricultural Census, the research studies how land titles are associated with different types of agrarian investments and explores how local customs and traditional land management systems shape these relationships. The project highlights that formal land-titling programs may support rural development, but also emphasizes the continuing importance of local institutions and community practices in shaping agricultural decision-making in rural areas.

UP Researcher: Esteban Colla De Robertis with Sandro Navarro-Castañeda, José M. Arranz, Mercedes Burguillo, Land Use Policy, 2021

Factors associated with harvest and postharvest loss among soybean farmers in Western Paraná State, Brazil

Reducing food loss is an important challenge for improving food security and agricultural sustainability. Large amounts of crops can be lost during harvesting and postharvest handling, yet relatively little is known about the farm-level factors that contribute to these losses. This project examines the causes of harvest and postharvest loss among soybean farmers in Paraná, one of Brazil’s largest agricultural regions. Using survey data from soybean producers, the research explores how farming practices, machinery operation, farmer education, and labor arrangements influence crop losses during harvesting. The study also investigates how incentive structures between farmers and machine operators affect agricultural efficiency and food loss.

UP Researcher: Akito Kamei with Mary Arends-Kuenning, Marcos Garcias, Pery Francisco Assis Shikida, Gisele Esser Romani Food Policy, 2022

Gender, Education, and Farm Succession in Western Paraná State, Brazil

Family farming plays an important role in food production and rural livelihoods in Brazil, yet demographic change and shifting educational opportunities are creating challenges for passing farms across generations. This study examines how gender norms, education, and household characteristics influence farm succession decisions among soybean farmers in Western Paraná, Brazil.

Using survey data from soybean-producing households, the research explores which families plan to transfer their farms to the next generation and how factors such as children’s gender, farm size, education, and access to government support programs shape these decisions. The findings highlight the importance of gender roles and rural development policies in determining the long-term continuity of family agriculture.

UP Researcher: Akito Kamei with Mary Arends-Kuenning, Marcos Garcias, Gisele Esser Romani, and Pery Francisco Assis Shikida Land Use Policy, 2021
